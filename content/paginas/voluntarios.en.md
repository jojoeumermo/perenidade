Everything here was written, reviewed and organised by people who believe knowledge should belong to everyone. There are many ways to help, and none of them requires a diploma.

## How to start

- **Without an account:** the [Contribute](/contribuir) page accepts lessons, suggested works, corrections and ideas from anyone. The curators read everything before it goes in.
- **As a volunteer:** fill in the [application](#candidatura) at the end of this page with your full name, a contact email, the role you want and a short introduction. You do not need an account. The coordination reads every application and replies to the email you gave; those who are accepted receive access to the panel there, already with the agreed role.

At this early stage, every publication is approved by the coordination.

## As a reviewer

This is the simplest way to start. While reading a lesson, you may find:

- a typo or a spelling mistake;
- wrong or outdated information;
- a confusing explanation that could be clearer.

Every lesson and every library entry has a **Suggest a correction** link at the end. In the panel, reviewers read what arrives and give their opinion before the curators decide.

## As an educator

Can you explain something? Write a lesson. Each educator looks after one or more subjects, and the name of whoever wrote the lesson appears on it.

- Write for beginners. Short sentences, everyday examples.
- One topic per lesson. Three short lessons are better than one long one.
- The text must be your own. Nothing copied from books, handouts or websites.
- **Every lesson needs at least one source:** a book, an article, an official document.
- By sending it, you publish under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, which guarantees your credit and the freedom to share.

Each track has a roadmap listing the lessons still to be written. Picking one of them is the best way to carry on the work from where it stopped.

## As a secondary educator

Not everyone can teach a whole subject, and that is fine. Secondary educators work alongside the educators: they publish useful materials, a stand-alone lesson, an exercise, a text that helps make sense of the topic. They have the same panel tools as educators, and what they publish appears in the subject, apart from the lessons of the course.

## As a mediator

The [Community](/comunidade) is open: anyone can read, and anyone who creates an account can ask and answer. The mediator facilitates that debate, follows how the rules are being kept and steps in when needed. The mediator acts on reports and on situations that call for human judgement, guides participants, asks for adjustments when appropriate and takes action against attacks, improper advertising and hate speech. The mediator also looks after the notices on the [Workers' Board](/mural) and gives a first reading to contributions coming from outside, before passing them on to the educators. When someone keeps crossing the line, the mediator can ban the account. Every decision is recorded and can be reviewed by the coordination.

## As a translator

The site exists in Portuguese, English, Spanish, Chinese and Russian, but most lessons are still in Portuguese only. If you are fluent in another language, translating a lesson opens the institute to millions of people. Every translation must be reviewed by someone fluent in the language.

## As a curator

The library only indexes: it keeps the title, the author, a synopsis and the link to the legitimate source. Curators suggest works in the public domain or under an open licence, check that old links still work and help decide what goes in.

## As a journalist, radio host and podcaster

The [Newspaper](/jornal) publishes news and analysis from the point of view of those who work. Checked facts, sources in plain sight and care when writing about people and organisations: the aim is to inform and to educate.

Those who write the journal also run [Rádio Lúcia](/radio): they set up channels and tracks, look after the live broadcast and suggest popular and community stations for the list on the radio page. They also produce the [podcasts](/podcasts): topic, script, recording, editing and publishing, with someone else reviewing before it goes on air.

## In tutoring

Anyone stuck on a subject can [ask for tutoring](/reforco). The request goes to the volunteers of that subject; whoever takes it answers by e-mail, patiently, showing the way and not just the answer. It is a good way to help if you have little time: one question at a time.

## As a designer

Lessons get better with good drawing: a diagram, a chart, a map, a poster to print. Designers answer requests from educators and from the newspaper, and prepare zines, leaflets and posters for the [materials](/materiais) page. Every image must be your own or freely licensed, with the credit in plain sight.

## In marketing

Brings design work and spreading the word together. People in marketing run the Instituto Lúcia Instagram: posts, stories, artwork and captions, always with checked sources and the institute's visual identity.

## As a developer

The site is free software and there is a lot to do: new practice tools, accessibility, performance on basic phones and slow connections, interface translations. Developers agree on the work with the community lead through the panel.

## In supervision

The supervisor sits just below the coordination and the vice-coordination, without their powers: a monitor of the panel's backstage, making sure things keep moving and do not fall into chaos when the coordination is away. The coordination entrusts supervision to someone who is already on the team.

## Spreading the word

Tell your colleagues, your union, your community prep course and your school about the institute. A teacher can print a lesson and take it to class. A study group can use a whole track. That is what it is for.

## Mission board

What the institute needs most right now:

- **Writing the missing lessons.** Each track shows what is "in preparation".
- **Reviewing published lessons**, checking dates, sums, examples and sources.
- **Translating** lessons into English, Spanish, Chinese and Russian.
- **Brazilian history from below:** the Malê Revolt, Canudos, the Revolt of the Lash, the general strike of 1917, the ABC strikes.
- **Suggesting free works** by Latin American, African and Asian authors.
- **Expanding the Memorial** and the **Glossary**, always with sources.
- **Checking rumours** for the [fact-checking](/checagens) section.

## Everything here is free

The institute charges nobody and is non-profit. Anyone who wants to help pay for the server can do so in [Support](/apoie), but what is most needed is your time and your knowledge.
