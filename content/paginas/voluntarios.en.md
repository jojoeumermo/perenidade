Everything here was written, reviewed and organised by people who believe knowledge should belong to everyone. There are many ways to help, and none of them requires a diploma.

## How to start

- **Without an account:** the [Contribute](/contribuir) page accepts lessons, suggested works, corrections and ideas from anyone. The team reads everything before it goes in.
- **As a volunteer:** fill in the [application](#candidatura) at the end of this page with your full name, a contact email, the role you want and a short introduction. You do not need an account. The coordination reads every application and replies to the email you gave; those who are accepted receive access to the panel there, already with the agreed role.

Each role sees in the panel only the tools for its own work, and everyone's desk shows, under **Where to start**, suggestions of what to do in that role: the next missing lesson in the subject, a track to translate, a subject with few works in the library. Nobody starts without knowing what to do.

Every publication is read by the team before it goes live.

## The roles

### Educator

Can you explain something? Write a lesson. Each educator looks after one or more subjects, and the author's name appears on the published lesson.

- Write for beginners. Short sentences, everyday examples.
- One topic per lesson. Three short lessons are better than one long one.
- The text must be your own. Nothing copied from books, handouts or websites.
- **Every lesson needs at least one source:** a book, an article, an official document.
- When you submit, you publish under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, which guarantees your credit and the freedom to share.

Each track has an outline with the lessons still missing. When a path is complete, the educator can propose the next topics: once approved, they enter the outline as "in preparation" and the path's goal grows. Educators also read the review queue of their subject, help in the community and answer tutoring requests.

### Secondary educator

Not everyone can teach a whole subject, but many people have something valuable to share. A secondary educator contributes to a subject alongside the tracks: a **stand-alone lesson**, an **exercise sheet**, a **summary**, a **study guide**, printable material or an annotated recommendation. These materials appear in their own tab on the subject page, **Stand-alone materials**, with the author's name.

A secondary educator has the same powers as an educator: if they wish, they can also write lessons for the tracks, review the subject's queue and answer tutoring requests. The same rules apply: their own text and at least one source.

### Reviewer

This is the simplest way to start. While reading a lesson, you may find:

- a typo or a spelling mistake;
- wrong or outdated information;
- a confusing explanation that could be clearer.

Every lesson and every entry has a **Suggest a correction** link at the end. In the panel, the reviewer reads what comes in and gives an opinion before the curators decide.

### Translator

The site exists in Portuguese, English, Spanish, Chinese and Russian, but most lessons are still only in Portuguese. If you master another language, translating a lesson opens the institute to millions of people. Every translation must be reviewed by someone who masters the language.

### Curator

The library only indexes: it keeps the title, the author, a synopsis and the link to the legitimate source, such as Project Gutenberg, the Marxists Internet Archive or MEC Livros, the digital library of Brazil's Ministry of Education. The curator suggests works in the public domain, with an open licence or free to borrow, checks that old links still work and decides, with the coordination, what goes on the site.

### Mediator

The [Community](/comunidade) is open: anyone can read, and anyone who creates an account can ask and answer. The mediator facilitates this debate, makes sure the rules are followed and steps in when needed. They handle reports and situations that call for human judgement, guide participants, ask for changes when appropriate and act against attacks, improper advertising and hate speech. They also look after the ads on the [Workers' Board](/mural) and give the first reading to contributions that come from outside, before passing them on to the educators. When someone keeps crossing the line, the mediator can ban the account. Every decision is recorded and can be reviewed by the coordination.

### Journalist, radio host and podcaster

The [Newspaper](/jornal) publishes news and analysis from the point of view of working people. Checked facts, visible sources and care when talking about people and organisations: the aim is to inform and to educate.

Those who make the newspaper also make [Rádio Lúcia](/radio) (channels, tracks, live broadcasts and the list of community stations) and the [podcasts](/podcasts): agenda, script, recording, editing and publishing. You can look after all three or just one.

### Podcast supervision

Before it goes live, every podcast episode goes through supervision: they listen, check facts and care, and approve it or send it back to production with a note. Nobody approves their own episode. The coordination chooses, within the podcast team, who does the supervision.

### Tutoring

Anyone who gets stuck on a subject can [ask for tutoring](/reforco). The request goes to the volunteers of that subject; whoever takes it answers by email, patiently, showing the way and not just the answer. It is a good way to help if you have little time: one question at a time.

### Designer

Lessons are better with good drawings: a diagram, a chart, a map, a poster to print. The designer answers requests from educators, the newspaper and the podcast and prepares zines, leaflets and posters for the [materials](/materiais) page. Every image sent must be your own or have a free licence, with the credit visible.

### Marketing and outreach

A free course only does its job if people know it exists. Marketing looks after outreach: posts and artwork for social media, call-out posters, volunteering and support campaigns, clips from podcast episodes. It works with design, uses the panel's materials and files and has its own channel on the team board.

### Development

The site is free software and there is a lot to do: new practice tools, accessibility, performance on simple phones and slow internet, translations of the interface. Programmers agree on the work with the coordination through the panel.

### Coordination and vice-coordination

The community coordination has the final word: it approves volunteers, assigns roles and subjects, decides what goes live and issues certificates. The vice-coordinator does almost everything the coordinator does and signs the certificates with them. These roles are not open to applications: they are entrusted to people who are already part of the team.

## Hours and certificate

Whoever accepts, in the panel, the volunteer agreement (based on Brazilian Law No. 9,608/1998: no salary and no employment relationship) has their hours counted by the platform itself for each finished task: approved lesson, review opinion, translation, answered tutoring request, published episode and others. Work done outside the panel can be logged by hand and is checked by the coordination.

Whenever they want, volunteers can request a certificate for a period. It carries three electronic signatures (an educator, the vice-coordinator and the coordinator) and a code that anyone can check on the [Check a certificate](/certificado) page. Until the institute is a registered organisation, the certificate is a statement from a community project: each school or university decides whether to accept it as complementary hours.

## Spreading the word

Tell your colleagues about the institute, in your union, in your community prep course, at your school. A teacher can print a lesson and take it to class. A study group can use a whole track. That is what it is for.

## Mission board

What the institute needs most right now:

- **Write the missing lessons.** Each track shows what is "in preparation".
- **Stand-alone materials** in every subject: exercise sheets, summaries and study guides.
- **Review the published lessons**, checking dates, calculations, examples and sources.
- **Translate** the lessons into English, Spanish, Chinese and Russian.
- **History of Brazil seen from below:** the Malê Revolt, Canudos, the Revolt of the Lash, the 1917 general strike, the ABC strikes.
- **Suggest free works** by Latin American, African and Asian authors.
- **Expand the Memorial** and the **Glossary**, always with sources.
- **Check rumours** for the [fact-checking](/checagens) section.
- **Spread the word** about the institute on social media and in social movements.

## Everything here is free

The institute charges nothing to anyone and is not for profit. Those who want to help pay for the server can do so at [Support](/apoie), but what is most needed is your time and your knowledge.
