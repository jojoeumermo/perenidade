The Lúcia Institute is a people's school and a free library on the internet: lessons from literacy to higher education, trades, a collection of free works, a journal, a podcast, tutoring and a community, all free of charge, without ads or trackers and under a free licence. Nobody pays to learn and nobody profits. Read [why we exist](/sobre) and [where the name comes from](/dedicatoria).

The institute is made by a team of volunteers: educators who write and record the lessons, reviewers, translators, curators, journalists, designers, community mediators, tutoring mentors and the people who look after the code. Every lesson is signed by its author and reviewed by colleagues before going live. [See how to join the team](/voluntarios).

## The founder

The institute was founded by **Jonathan Santos**, raised in the Morro do Urubu complex in Rio de Janeiro's North Zone. He studied at Colégio Pedro II, where he took the technical course in Systems Development, and received an *honoris causa* bachelor's degree in Sciences and Letters. Fluent in English and a student of Chinese, he studies Computer Science and Computer Networks and works at one of the largest schools in the country.

Before the institute, he taught computing at a charity and registered and referred people in economic vulnerability to a programme of affordable eyeglasses. The institute grows out of that experience: knowledge is a tool of emancipation, and it must reach, free and unlocked, those who study on the bus after work, phone in hand.
