We wake up early. We take the packed bus. We work all day and, when there is strength left, we study at night.

They told us studying is a luxury. That health care means a queue. That the culture of the favela is a police matter. That this is how it is and how it will always be.

It is not. And it will not be.

Instituto Lúcia is a popular school and a free library: lessons, a library catalogue, a newspaper, a radio and a podcast, free of charge, with no ads and no padlock. But for us, studying does not end on the screen. We study to understand the world. And we study to change it.

**Educate yourselves, agitate, organise.**

The name is a tribute: see the [dedication](/dedicatoria).

## We believe

- **Knowledge belongs to the people.** Everything humanity has learned was made by generations of working women and men. It cannot be sold back to the people who made it.
- **Knowledge is not a commodity.** Nobody should pay to learn. Nobody should profit from what belongs to everyone.
- **No right fell from the sky.** What the people have, the people won by fighting. And what is not defended is lost.
- **Nobody frees themselves alone.** Learning together is organising solidarity.
- **The truth has a source.** Whatever we say, the reader can check. We do not spread lies, not even about those who attack us.
- **We have a side.** We are on the left and we do not hide it. We stand with those who work, with those who fight for land, a roof, wages and freedom, and with every people that resists exploitation and oppression.

## What we fight for

### 1. Education comes first

Everything starts here. Brazil's Constitution says education is a right of all and a duty of the State, and that public education is free ([arts. 205 and 206](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). On paper, it is written. In life, there is no school near home, no teacher, no place, no time.

We want a good public school in every neighbourhood, a public university open to those who work by day and live far away, valued teachers and students who can afford to keep studying. And we want popular education: the kind that starts from the learner's life and teaches you to read the world, not just to pass the exam.

Here nobody pays and nobody needs an account to learn. **Learn for free. Contribute if you want. Nothing is lost.**

### 2. Favela culture is knowledge

Funk, rap, slam, pixo, graffiti, rap battles, poetry nights, dances, photo and video collectives. The periphery creates every day, and is almost always treated as a problem.

For us it is language, history and knowledge. We want public money for culture in the favela, places to rehearse and to perform, and creators who own what they create. The Constitution guarantees everyone access to culture ([art. 215](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). Everyone includes the favela.

### 3. Public health: the SUS is ours

Health is a right of all and a duty of the State, with universal and equal access ([art. 196](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). That has a name: the SUS, Brazil's public health system.

We defend a public SUS, free and for everyone. With a clinic near home, shorter queues, care for mental health and respect for those who work in it. Health is not a business.

### 4. Decent work and income

On 16 October 2026, Brazil's Congress promulgated the amendment that ends the 6x1 schedule (six days of work for one of rest): two days of rest a week and shorter hours, with no pay cut, in a 14-month transition ([CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/politica/congresso-promulga-pec-que-acaba-com-a-escala-6x1/)). It did not fall from the sky.

Now it has to be enforced. Real rest, wages that last until the end of the month, inspection of those who break the rules, rights for app workers and an end to all slave labour. Income must stay with those who work.

### 5. Housing and the city

Housing is a social right ([art. 6](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). A house is for living in, not for speculation.

We want sanitation, upgrading without evictions, squares, sports courts, libraries and street lights. The city belongs to those who live in it, not only to those who profit from it.

### 6. Transport

Transport is a social right too ([art. 6](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). Expensive fares and packed buses trap us in the neighbourhood: far from school, work, the clinic and leisure.

We fight for good, cheap public transport and, on the horizon, free transport. Those who study must be able to get there.

### 7. Indigenous peoples and quilombolas

The Constitution recognises the right of Indigenous peoples to the lands they traditionally occupy and orders the Union to demarcate them ([art. 231](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)). To quilombola communities (the remaining communities of the quilombos), it guarantees definitive ownership of their lands ([art. 68 of the transitional provisions](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)).

Demarcation now. Land titles now. Respect for each people's language, school and way of life. Those who were here first cannot be the last to have rights.

### 8. Anti-racist struggle

Racism decides who gets stopped in the street, who gets followed in the shop and who does not get called for the job. It divides the working class and cheapens the lives of part of it.

We fight it at school, at work, in the street and at home. The history of Black people is the history of Brazil, and it is taught with pride.

### 9. Feminist struggle

Equal pay. An end to violence against women. Childcare for those who need to work and study. Shared care for the home and the children.

Sexism shuts doors every day. We open them.

### 10. LGBTQIAPN+ pride

Living, loving and studying without fear. Your name respected at roll call. Schools without LGBTphobia. Work for those thrown out of home and school.

Nobody is left behind for being who they are.

### 11. The environment

Everyone has the right to a balanced environment, and defending it is the duty of the government and of all of us ([art. 225](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)).

The bill for destruction cannot fall on those who destroyed the least. Clean rivers, trees in the street, standing forests and water for all.

### 12. People's media

The big media have owners, and the truth of the periphery, the countryside and the villages rarely gets through. So we make our own: newspaper, radio and podcast, with sources and open archives.

And we do it without tracking anyone. Your data is not for sale.

### 13. Organising and struggle in the street

The screen helps, but it is not enough. The struggle happens where the people are: in the neighbourhood, at school, at work, in the occupation, in the culture circle.

That is why there are [study groups](/nucleos) that meet close to home. That is why there is the [Lúcia Revolutionary Front](/escola/manifesto), which meets in person and goes to the street. Nobody occupies a territory alone.

## What we do

- **[Lessons](/trilhas)** from primary school to university, short, with a quiz and the sources in view. And [video lessons](/videoaulas) recorded by our educators, with transcripts.
- **[Library](/acervo)**: an index of free works, with a link to the legitimate source. No third-party files are hosted here.
- **[Newspaper](/jornal), [Radio](/radio) and [podcasts](/podcasts)**: people's media, against the media monopoly.
- **[Study nuclei](/nucleos)**: people who study together, in the neighbourhood, the union, the occupation, the school.
- **[Territories](/territorios)**: culture and struggle collectives from the favela that walk with the institute.
- **[Workers' Wall](/mural)**: self-employed workers advertise for free, with no fee and no middleman.
- **[Labs](/laboratorios)** and practice tools that run on your own device.
- **[Lúcia Maria Revolutionary School](/escola)**: the training home for those who decide to become activists.

Everything is reviewed before it goes online, and every lesson cites at least one source. Anyone can [contribute](/contribuir). The history keeps every version: if something wrong is published, we go back to the previous one.

We use no points, rankings or tricks to hook anyone. People study here out of revolt, curiosity and hope.

## Come with us

If you are a student, a worker, someone from the favela, the countryside or the village, and you do not accept that the world stays as it is: this place is yours.

Study. Teach. Write. Translate. Draw. Code. Organise your neighbourhood. See how to join in [Volunteering](/voluntarios). If you can, help keep the site online at [Support](/apoie). Donating is optional, and access is the same for everyone.

**Educate yourselves, agitate, organise. Lúcia, presente!**

### Privacy

There is no sign-up to study. The lessons you complete, your chosen theme, your notes and your drafts stay only in your browser. In [My data](/meus-dados) you can download or delete everything.

An account is optional and is for those who want to talk in the community or volunteer. In that case we keep your email and the name you choose to show; team members have a few more data points, all listed in the [privacy policy](/privacidade). You can delete your account whenever you want. The site uses no tracking cookies and no ads.

### Copyright

Only works in the public domain, under an open licence or written by the community itself are included here. Copyrighted books appear only as references, with a link to the publisher or a library. If something is wrong, [report it](/denunciar): the item is taken down while it is reviewed.
