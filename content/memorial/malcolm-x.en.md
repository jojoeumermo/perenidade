Born Malcolm Little, he lost his father as a child and went to prison as a young man. There he studied, converted to Islam and, once released, became the best-known voice of the Nation of Islam, defending Black pride and the right to self-defence. In 1964 he broke with the organisation, made the pilgrimage to Mecca and came to treat racism as a human rights issue, linking the struggle of Black Americans to the liberation struggles of Africa. He was shot dead on 21 February 1965 as he began a speech in New York.
